# Consulting market — the category and its competitors

The software, management and AI consulting category Missing Bulb sells into, the
competitor tiers it is measured against, and the pricing and engagement norms a
small firm is read against.

## Key insights

- AI consulting is roughly $14bn in 2026 and forecast to compound about 26% a year — the category is not saturated.
- SMBs are the faster-growing segment, because the Fortune 500 already built internal AI teams.
- Benchmark pricing for small-business AI work: $5k–$25k per project, or $150–$350 per hour.
- Outcome-based and capped-budget engagements beat hourly; shipping in weeks beats months of discovery.
- Nobody sells "AI Software Management" — the recognised neighbours are AI strategy, governance and policy.
- Boutiques win on cost, speed and hands-on build, not breadth: that is the wedge against IBM and RSM.

## Category as stated

Missing Bulb places itself in software consulting, management consulting, and AI
consulting, plus the intersection it calls AI Software Management consulting. At
adoption the owner explicitly left the competitor set unnamed. This pass named it.

## The competitor set

There is no single competitor list, and that turned out to be the useful finding:
the three named practices are sold by structurally different firms, so "who
competes with Missing Bulb" resolves per practice, not once.

**Tier 1 — global and mid-market generalists.** [IBM
Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence), [RSM
US](https://rsmus.com/services/digital-transformation/artificial-intelligence.html)
and [Centric Consulting](https://centricconsulting.com/technology-solutions/artificial-intelligence-consulting/)
sell AI strategy, governance, integration and "responsible scaling" as part of
broader digital-transformation practices. They are not realistically competed
with on breadth, credentials or procurement comfort.

**Tier 2 — AI-specialist boutiques.** The firms catalogued in industry roundups
([LeewayHertz](https://www.leewayhertz.com/top-ai-consulting-companies/),
[Intellectyx](https://www.intellectyx.com/list-of-20-leading-ai-consulting-firms-providing-comprehensive-services/),
[Binariks](https://binariks.com/blog/top-ai-consulting-firms/)) are the actual
comparison set. The research is consistent that boutiques compete on **lower
cost, faster execution, and more hands-on technical work** — not on coverage.

This is the wedge, and it is a real one for a firm of Missing Bulb's size and
shape: a small team that ships working code quickly is competing on precisely the
axis where boutiques are documented to beat the majors, rather than trying to
look like a small IBM.

## The label

"AI Software Management" appears **nowhere** as an established category name. The
recognised adjacent offerings, consistent across the majors' own service pages,
are: AI strategy and governance, AI policy management, AI implementation and
integration, and responsible scaling. So the label is Missing Bulb's coinage.

That is not automatically a problem — a named category the firm defines can become
the evaluation standard buyers judge others by, which the trust-signal research
notes is stronger positioning than case studies alone. But it only works if the
site *defines* the term rather than assuming it lands. And per the [brand
page](../brand/README.md), Missing Bulb already has the artefact that defines it:
Claudinite is a working system for managing AI-assisted software development.
Coining the category and shipping the thing it names is a much better position
than coining it alone.

## Pricing and engagement norms

Benchmarks a small firm will be read against: **$5,000–$25,000 per project**, or
**$150–$350 per hour**, for AI consulting aimed at small businesses. Pricing is
not usually published in this category — "contact us" is the norm, which is worth
knowing precisely because it means publishing a starting number is a differentiator
rather than a faux pas.

On structure, the research is directional: firms winning in 2026 use
**outcome-based, phased, or capped-budget** engagements rather than hourly billing,
and **deliver a working system in weeks rather than months of discovery**. Both
favour a small team over a large one.

## Market size

The AI consulting services market is put at roughly **$14.1bn in 2026**, growing
to **$116.81bn by 2035** at a **CAGR of about 26.5%**. The segment detail matters
more than the headline: **SMB AI consulting is growing at about 25.7% annually,
outpacing the enterprise segment**, on the reasoning that Fortune 500 companies
already have internal AI teams while smaller businesses are starting out. A small
consultancy is therefore aimed at the faster-growing half of a fast-growing
category — which is a positioning argument the site can legitimately make.
